Academic Theory & Methodological Rationale

Why Hybrid Machine Learning Classification?

In cardiovascular disease prediction, single machine learning algorithms exhibit unique structural limitations and structural inductive biases.

Single-Model Limitations

Logistic Regression assumes linear separability; Decision Trees overfit clinical noise; SVM is sensitive to margin hyperparameter calibration; KNN suffers from curse of dimensionality.

Soft Voting Formulation

p(y=1|x) = ∑ [w_m × p_m(y=1|x)] / ∑ w_m

Weighted probability aggregation smooths out individual outlier predictions, allowing strong posterior signals to prevail.

Empirical Evidence

On our Cleveland dataset evaluation, the Hybrid Ensemble delivers 86.89% test accuracy and 81.38% 5-fold cross-validation accuracy, providing more stable performance across patient variations.

Academic Integrity & Scientific Transparency Statement

We do not claim that hybrid ensemble classification universally outperforms every possible single algorithm in every scenario. As seen in our live evaluation, Random Forest individually scores 90.16% on this specific test partition, while the Hybrid Ensemble achieves 86.89% with higher consensus stability and lower cross-validation standard error. Ensembling provides clinical safety: reducing individual model volatility when exposed to unseen distribution shifts in patient vitals.
